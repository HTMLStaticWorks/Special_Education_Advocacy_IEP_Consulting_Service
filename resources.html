Parent Guidance

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions caregivers ask about IEP meetings, accommodations, and evaluations.

An IEP (Individualized Education Program) is governed by IDEA law and provides specialized instruction, individualized learning goals, and related services for students qualifying under specific disability categories. A 504 Plan is governed by civil rights law (Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act) and provides accommodations to ensure equal access to general education for students with impairment, without specialized instruction.

Always submit evaluation requests in writing (email or letter) addressed to the school principal or special education coordinator. State clearly that you are requesting a Full and Individual Initial Evaluation (FIIE) under IDEA, detail your areas of concern (e.g., reading fluency, attention, speech), and request the consent forms to initiate the evaluation timeline.

If you disagree with the findings or recommendations of a district evaluation, you have the right to request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense under IDEA regulations. The district must either agree to fund an independent evaluator or initiate due process to defend its own evaluation.

Yes. Federal IDEA regulations explicitly allow parents to bring individuals with knowledge or special expertise regarding the child — including independent educational advocates and consultants — to any IEP team meeting, whether held in-person or virtually.
